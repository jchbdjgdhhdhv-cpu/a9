Trouser Hem Guide

Trouser length is the single biggest thing a tailor can fix for you. Here is how to choose a break and measure a length that works with your shoes.

Before you start: wash and dry new cotton trousers once before hemming; they can shrink by a few centimetres.

Step 1

Wear the right shoes

Always measure trousers with the shoes you will wear them with. A loafer and a chunky boot need different lengths. Stand naturally, weight on both feet, and look straight ahead rather than down.

Step 2

Choose your break

  • No break: the hem just kisses the shoe. Modern and clean, best with slim, tapered trousers.
  • Quarter break: a slight dimple at the front. The safest, most versatile choice.
  • Half break: one soft fold. Classic for suits and straight-leg trousers.
  • Full break: a deep fold. Traditional, and suits wide trousers.
Step 3

Mind the leg width

The narrower the leg, the less break it can carry before it bunches. A wide leg can take more length and drape gracefully. As a guide, the back of the hem can sit a little lower than the front, about halfway down the heel counter.

Step 4

Turn-ups or plain

Turn-ups (cuffs) of about 3.5–4.5 cm add weight and help trousers hang straight. They suit wider, heavier trousers; plain hems are cleaner and more formal on slim cuts.

Step 5

Pin, then press

Fold the hem up to the chosen length and pin all the way round. Check in a mirror, take a few steps, sit down and stand up again. Press the fold before sewing, and leave enough allowance in case you ever want to lengthen.
