Hemline Guide

Where a skirt or dress ends changes how the whole outfit looks. Here is how to pick a hemline and the finish that suits the fabric.

Before you start: keep the lining 2–3 cm shorter than the outer fabric so it never peeks out.

Step 1

Know the lengths

NameWhere it ends
MiniMid-thigh
Above the kneeA hand’s width above the knee
KneeJust at or below the kneecap
MidiMid-calf
Tea lengthJust above the ankle
MaxiAnkle or floor
Step 2

Find your narrow points

Hemlines tend to look best where the leg is slimmest: just above or below the knee, the narrowest part of the calf, or the ankle. A hem that ends at the widest part of the calf can make the leg look heavier.

Step 3

Match the shoes

Midi and tea lengths look balanced with a heel or a shoe that shows some of the foot. Flat, chunky shoes pair well with shorter or ankle-length hems.

Step 4

Choose the finish

Heavier fabrics take a blind hem of 4–5 cm, which adds weight and swing. Light, floaty fabrics need a narrow rolled hem. Circle and bias-cut skirts should hang for a day before hemming, because the fabric drops unevenly.

Step 5

Level it

Have someone measure up from the floor all the way round while you stand in your shoes, marking with chalk. This gives an even hem even when the garment hangs unevenly.
